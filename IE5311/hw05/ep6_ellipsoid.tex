{\large\bfseries EP6.\quad The ellipsoid method for linear programming}\\[1pt]
{\small Lidivine Kengne \quad\textbar\quad IE 5311-001 (D01), Homework 5}
\vspace{0.3em}

\textbf{1. The main idea.}
The method never reads a constraint matrix. It is handed a convex set
$K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ through a \emph{separation oracle}: a
routine that, for any query $z$, either certifies $z \in K$ or returns
$d \neq 0$ with $d^{\!\top}x < d^{\!\top}z$ for all $x \in K$. That is
the whole interface, and the whole point: $K$ may have exponentially
many facets, none ever written down. Write $E(z,Q) = \{x : (x-z)^{\!\top}Q^{-1}(x-z) \le 1\}$ with
$Q \succ 0$ and start from any $E_0 \supseteq K$. Query the oracle at
the centre $z_k$: if $z_k \in K$ the search ends, otherwise the
returned $d$ confines $K$ to the half ellipsoid
$E_k \cap \{x : d^{\!\top}x \le d^{\!\top}z_k\}$, and $E_{k+1}$ is the
smallest ellipsoid containing that. With
$g = Q_k d / \sqrt{d^{\!\top} Q_k d}$,
\[
  z_{k+1} = z_k - \frac{g}{n+1},
  \qquad
  Q_{k+1} = \frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}
            \Bigl( Q_k - \frac{2}{n+1}\, g\,g^{\!\top} \Bigr),
\]
a rank-one update costing $O(n^2)$ arithmetic. The volume contracts by
a fixed factor whatever the cut, so a point of $K$ is found within
$O(n \log(V/v))$ calls when $\vol(K) \ge v$ and $\vol(E_0) \le V$. That
factor is usually quoted as $e^{-1/(2(n+1))}$, but that is only a
bound. Since
$g^{\!\top}Q_k^{-1}g = 1$ by construction,
$\det\bigl(Q_k - \tfrac{2}{n+1}gg^{\!\top}\bigr)
 = \det Q_k \cdot \tfrac{n-1}{n+1}$, so
\[
  \frac{\vol(E_{k+1})}{\vol(E_k)}
  = \Bigl[\Bigl(\tfrac{n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\Bigr)^{\! n}
          \tfrac{n-1}{n+1}\Bigr]^{1/2},
\]
a constant attained \emph{exactly} at every step, whatever the cut.
For $n = 3$ it is $27/32 = 0.84375$ against the bound
$e^{-1/8} = 0.8825$. The contraction still weakens as $n$ grows ---
each cut discards about a $1/n$ fraction --- which is why the method
is slow.

\textbf{2. Historical significance: linear programming is in P.}
For an LP with rational data of bit size $L$, a nonempty feasible
region can be perturbed to have volume at least $2^{-O(nL)}$ and lies
in a ball of radius $2^{O(L)}$, so the trivial oracle ``scan the rows,
return a violated one'' gives $O(n^2 L)$ iterations. Khachiyan's 1979
proof was the first of its kind and a surprise: simplex was then as
now the only algorithm anyone ran, and it is exponential in the worst
case (Klee and Minty's distorted cube forces Dantzig's rule through
all $2^n$ vertices).

\textbf{3. The lasting significance: separation equals optimization.}
Because the method touches $K$ only through the oracle, its running
time depends on the number of \emph{variables} and the cost of one
separation, never on the number of constraints. Gr\"otschel, Lov\'asz
and Schrijver turned this into an equivalence: for a well-described
polyhedron, optimizing a linear function over it and separating from
it are polynomial-time equivalent. An exponential family of
constraints is therefore no obstacle, given a polynomial oracle.

\textbf{4. Where this course has already used the idea.}
The DFJ subtour constraints number $2^{|V|}$, and the lazy callback
written for Homework~1 is a separation oracle for them, finding the
violated subtour by a minimum cut. Homework~5 is the same idea from
the other side: the Dantzig--Wolfe master holds one column per
pattern, so its \emph{dual} holds one row per pattern, and the pricing
knapsack is precisely the separation oracle for that dual. Column
generation is a cutting-plane method on the dual, and the ellipsoid
method would solve the same relaxation in polynomial time from the
same oracle --- which is how one proves such a relaxation tractable
despite exponentially many variables.

\textbf{5. What it costs, measured.}
Run on the Part~1 LP, with the oracle scanning the rows of
$Ax \le b$, $x \ge 0$ and $c^{\!\top}x \ge z^\star - \delta$, starting
from a ball of radius $20$ (\texttt{ep6\_ellipsoid.py}):

\setlength{\tabcolsep}{7pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$\delta$ & oracle calls & $c^{\!\top}x$ found & observed ratio/step\\
\midrule
$10^{0}$  &  29 & 23.807686 & 0.843750\\
$10^{-1}$ &  69 & 23.942739 & 0.843750\\
$10^{-2}$ & 105 & 23.992071 & 0.843750\\
$10^{-3}$ & 158 & 23.999703 & 0.843750\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The ratio is $0.84375$ to six figures every run, as the derivation
requires. Since $\vol(K) \sim \delta^{n}$, a decade of $\delta$ costs a
factor $10^{3}$ of volume: $40.7$ calls at the exact rate, $55.3$ at
the bound, against $40, 36, 53, 24$ observed. The bound overstates the
work by about a third and the method is still hopeless --- four
digits cost $182$ oracle calls in $\mathbb{R}^3$, where the simplex of
Part~2 reached $x^\star = (4,4,4)$ exactly in $3$ pivots. Its standing
is as an existence proof and a reduction tool.
